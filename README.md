# Skirmish

Skirmish is a top-down multiplayer arena shooter for the browser. Its gameplay is modeled on gats.io. The name, art and code are original.

## Run

```bash
npm install
npm start
```

Open http://localhost:8080. Set `PORT` to change the port. Accounts and stats are saved in `data/`.

## Play

Pick a weapon, a color and an armor tier, then choose an FFA, TDM, DOM or BR room (see Last Squad below), or open a zombies squad (see Zombies below). Bots keep every room at 18 players or more. In TDM and DOM a team short of humans gets three bots for each human it lacks, since humans carry four times the health. In TDM and DOM every body wears its team color.

Each room rotates through four maps, each 6000 px square: Causeway (three lanes split by long walls with crossings, round a planter courtyard), Plaza (city blocks round a wide open square with a fountain at its heart), Old Town (a quarter of small rooms with a doorway in every wall between two long streets, round a planted courtyard) and Quarry (long open avenues beside walled pockets, round a monolith of four sandstone blocks). Every mode loads the next map when a round restarts. TDM is won at 150 team kills or, after 12 minutes, by the team ahead on score and then on kills. DOM is won at 3000 points or, after 15 minutes, by the team ahead. An FFA round runs 10 minutes and goes to the player with the most kills, bot or human, fewest deaths breaking a tie; a human who reaches 30 kills ends it early and leads the podium. A timed round that nobody wins, such as an FFA round with no kills or a dead-even TDM round, restarts at once on the next map. The objective line and the FFA leaderboard count the round's time down. The next map is announced 15 seconds ahead.

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move |
| Mouse | Aim |
| Left click | Fire |
| R | Reload |
| Space | Use ability (its chip under your health counts the cooldown down, pulses gold when it is back, and flashes red if you press too early) |
| 1-9, 0 | Pick a perk or an evolution (or click its tile) |
| B | Zombies: build mode by day (left click builds, right click takes your wall down) |
| E | Zombies: hold to revive a downed squadmate or repair a wall or the Bastion |
| N | Zombies: by day, ready for night; night falls once every living human is ready |
| Tab | Hold for the whole leaderboard |
| Enter | Chat |
| M | Mute sound |

The world wears the same kit as the interface, so nothing looks borrowed from another art set: every solid, body and gun is a flat shape in one muted military palette with an ink outline and two hard cel-shading steps (a light edge toward the light, a dark one away from it), shadows are crisp drop shadows, the floor is calm bone concrete in big slabs, concrete blocks are riveted gunmetal plates, sandstone is khaki plate, planters and crates are olive, the arena is framed in the interface's orange hazard tape, and the horde wears sickly versions of the same olive, khaki, steel and rust. The interface wears one field kit throughout, menu and HUD alike: matte gunmetal plates with clipped corners and an orange corner bracket, bone-white Barlow Condensed type (self-hosted in `public/fonts`, under the SIL Open Font License), and a single signal orange for whatever you can act on or have chosen. Guns in the loadout, the evolve picks, the kill feed, in every player's hands and on the ground beside corpses are the same detailed, shaded side-profile vector art (`src/client/gunart.ts`): each class has its own receiver, stock, grip, magazine, sights and furniture, and every evolution reshapes its class's art by its look (a longer barrel, over-under barrels, a twin pair, an accent stripe). Each class gun's card in the loadout shows power, rate, reach and mobility as bars against the other classes, callouts land as stamped plates, and the death card is edged in hazard tape.

On a phone the game plays on its side: Play asks for fullscreen and a landscape lock where the browser allows it, and a phone held upright is asked to turn sideways. The left thumb walks and the right thumb aims and fires, each wherever it lands on its half of the screen; faint rings labelled Move and Aim · Fire show where until the sticks are used, and, as in mobile shooters, the reload and ability buttons arc above the aim stick's resting spot, where the thumb reaches for them on purpose but never lands on them while aiming; the minimap moves to the top left and the chat to the top centre, out of both thumbs' way. The buttons are icons rather than letters: reload is a circular arrow that fills round as the reload runs and glows red on an empty mag, and the ability button shows the ability's own icon with a cooldown sweep and the seconds left, gold when ready, or a lock and the unlocking score before one is picked. Touch screens get larger HUD text, and the HUD shrinks no further than 90% on a small screen. The level-up dock opens collapsed to a pulsing pill so it never covers the fight until tapped. Touch aim has a light assist: when an enemy's lead point, found the same way the bots lead, lies within 7 degrees of the thumb's aim, the aim turns toward it by at most 2.5 degrees. `node scripts/aim-assist-bench.ts` measures it at about 20% more hits for a thumb that leads poorly, almost all of it at range.

A round leaves the muzzle at five times its cruise speed and eases down over its first couple of hundred px, so up close it lands before anyone can step aside, even a target already strafing, while at range most rounds fly slowly enough to see coming and sidestep; sniper rounds cruise fast but get a gentler kick out of the muzzle, so a sniper does not also own close range. Pistols and SMGs are a little more accurate standing still than walking, and rifles, machine guns and snipers much more so; shotguns fire as true on the run, and a planted sniper has no spread at all. Assault rifles, SMGs, machine guns and the Hailstorm bloom when held: past the first few rounds of a spray each shot widens the spread, so short bursts stay tight, and it cools once you pause. Bloom is a rule between players: in Zombies a held trigger stays steady. Each round is drawn as a short solid slug in its gun's bullet color with a thin highlight and a faint trail, dark by day and light at night. Bots play by the same rules: they aim where the round will meet a moving target, found from the target's velocity and the round's flight along the muzzle curve, with a slightly different judgment of the lead each time they engage, their aim widens when suppressed, and rounds cracking past make them sidestep and duck like a hit would. An enemy round passing within 60 px of you suppresses you: your spread widens by up to 25%, a planted sniper loses its pinpoint aim, the edges of your screen close in under a heavy dark vignette that hides them almost entirely at full suppression, and the HUD switches to its high-contrast colors so you can still read your ammo. Each round adds what its gun suppresses, and suppression holds only 0.15 seconds before it fades, so only sustained automatic fire keeps it building: an LMG pins you down in under a second and its shadow lingers for about a second and a half after it stops, an assault rifle or SMG does so in one to two seconds, while a pistol barely builds any and a sniper's crack is shaken off in about half a second. Heavier armor and heavier guns slow you down: light, medium and heavy armor cost 10%, 20% and 30% of your speed, and a machine gun about a quarter more, but no loadout drops below 58% of base speed; the Lightweight attachment gives 25% back. A grenade, mine or other blast shakes your screen, harder the closer and bigger it is, and big guns (shotguns, rifles, hand cannons, machine guns) kick the camera back along your aim when you fire. A fallen player lies where they died for 30 seconds, a grey body crossed out with an X, knocked back along the killing shot, in a pool of blood sprayed out the far side, with their gun slid to a random spot and angle beside them; an explosive death leaves a darker, scorched body on soot with the gun flung further. Corpses fade out over the last 3 seconds, and a new map clears the field. In Zombies every zombie the squad kills stays where it fell until the night ends, eyes crossed out and limbs splayed in a pool of ichor sprayed away from whoever killed it; a pile of them merges into one outlined heap, and the whole field fades at dawn.

Points in one life raise your level. At 100, 420 and 620 points you pick an attachment, a survival perk and an ability. At 250 and 1000 points your gun evolves into one of two branches: 6 class guns, 12 stage-1 guns and 24 stage-2 guns. Dying resets your score, perks and gun to the class gun. While your level is below the average level of the other living players, every point you earn counts 1.5 times. A player holding a stage-2 gun is hunted: every enemy minimap pings their position every 2.5 seconds and whenever they fire an unsilenced shot, the kill feed announces it, and killing them pays a 300-point bounty on top of the kill. An attacker who dealt at least 30% of a victim's max health earns a 50-point assist when someone else gets the kill. A player killed by their own blast gives the kill to whoever hurt them most in the last 10 seconds.

A kill refuels the killer on the spot: 35% of their max health back and half a mag (not mid-reload), so winning a fight carries you into the next instead of sending you off to wait out the regen delay. Kills in one life make a streak. From three kills a flame and the count show beside your name for everyone, and ending a streak of five or more pays a 250-point shutdown bonus. Whoever killed you last is your nemesis, marked over their head when you see them; killing them back pays 125 for revenge and settles it. Kills within 4 seconds of each other chain into double, triple and quad kills, and streak milestones are named at 3, 5, 8, 12 and 20. The kill sting climbs two semitones a kill up the streak, and a kill punches the camera and rings out from the crosshair. The death card sums up the life you lost (kills, damage, level and time survived), stamps each stat that beat this browser's record, and names your new nemesis.

Medals are where most of a life's score now comes from, so the ladder above sits higher than a bare kill count would need (zombie kills pay 1.6 times to keep pace). The moment you earn one it punches onto the screen above centre in the manner of Call of Duty: a struck metal medal (`src/client/medals.ts`, one SVG per medal on a bronze, silver, gold or platinum blank with ribbon, laurel or wings) flashes, a shine sweeps it, its name and score land under it with a bell for its metal, and medals earned together line up side by side. Each pays its score on top of the kill:

| Medal | For | Score |
|---|---|---|
| First Blood | the round's first kill | 125 |
| Double, Triple, Quad Kill, Massacre | 2, 3, 4, 5+ kills chained within 4 s | 60, 125, 250, 400 |
| On Fire, Rampage, Unstoppable, Untouchable, Legendary | 3, 5, 8, 12, 20 kills in one life | 60, 125, 250, 400, 600 |
| Long Shot | a kill from 650 px or more | 75 |
| Point Blank | a kill from within 90 px | 50 |
| Clutch | a kill on your attacker while under 20% health | 125 |
| Close Call | living 6 s after falling under 10% health | 75 |
| Revenge | killing your nemesis | 125 |
| Shutdown | ending a streak of 5 or more | 250 |
| Bounty | killing a hunted player | 300 |
| Ghost | covering 3000 px in one life without firing a shot | 75 |

Every name has a profile that lasts across lives and matches (`src/server/profiles.ts`, saved to `data/profiles.json`): career kills, deaths, matches, best streak, distance walked and how often each medal was earned. Lifetime medals are rungs on 16 career tracks (Centurion for career kills, Marksman for Long Shots, Marathon for km walked, Phantom for Ghost medals and so on), each with bronze, silver, gold and platinum tiers; crossing a rung pays 100, 200, 400 or 800 points at once and lands a bigger "Lifetime medal" toast. A player wears their rarest lifetime medal as a badge beside their name in every match. `profile.html?name=...` (linked from the name field, the footer and a human killer on the death card) shows a name's worn medal, career numbers, every track with its progress to the next rung, and every match medal with its count; `GET /api/profile/:name` serves the same data. A signed-in account owns its name and its profile: registering a name wipes whatever guests left under it, a bot or guest holding the name when its owner joins is renamed, a guest can never add to an account's profile, and a second session of the same account still credits the account. Guests sharing an unregistered name share its profile.

### Zombies

Zombies is a co-op mode for a private squad of up to four. Start a squad from the menu's Zombies block and send friends its invite link (`?squad=<code>`), which opens the menu with that squad selected. Under the hood `POST /api/squads` opens a squad room and answers `{ "room": "z-xxxxxx" }`; everyone who joins the WebSocket with `?room=<code>` plays in it. Squads stay off the public room list, one address may open six a minute, at most 20 run at once, and a squad closes 30 seconds after its last human leaves. Bots fill the empty seats and give a seat up when a human joins. Humans keep their fourfold health. The whole squad is one team, and nothing the squad does hurts the squad.

The squad defends the Bastion, a core at the center of the Outpost map with 50 survivors inside. A run opens on a 40-second day, which ends early once every living human presses N. Night follows, and a horde walks in toward the core from the map edges the night table names. Later nights send their packs in waves: night N sends N-1 packs at once, then waits as long as those packs would have taken one at a time. By day the HUD forecasts tonight with the deadliest kinds first and the horde's size for your squad, for example `Tonight · Brutes, walkers and runners from the east · 37 strong`. The night ends once the whole horde has spawned and died. First light comes 90 seconds after the last pack walks in and burns whatever is still out for no scrap, so a zombie that cannot reach anything never holds the night; the phase line counts down to it. `NIGHTS` in `src/shared/defs.ts` lists ten nights, one row each, with each kind's count for a squad of four bots and its sides; night 5 brings a Colossus and night 10, the Tide, brings every kind from every side. A human counts as one and a half bots toward the horde's size. A boss, a kind that walks alone like the Colossus, comes once for any squad and has its health scaled by the squad's share instead. Holding through the Tide wins the run: `The Bastion held. N survivors saw the morning.` The core's fall, or the loss of its last survivor, loses it. A report of the night reached, the run's length, each player's kills, revives and buildings put up, and the kills of the turrets and the Bastion shows for 20 seconds, and then a fresh run starts on a reset map.

Every 100 hp of harm the core takes costs one survivor, however well the core is mended between bites, so repairing the core keeps it standing but raises no one. At night the HUD shows tonight's dead beside the survivor count. At dawn each survivor left pays 3 scrap. The survivors also shoot from the core's walls at zombies within 220 px, slower as they die.

A walker bites a squad player it can see within 160 px. Runners are small and fast, come in packs and chase the squad from 360 px. Plated walkers shrug 10 damage off every bullet, so small rounds barely scratch them while heavy rounds, piercing rounds and blasts get through. A bloater bursts where it dies, hurting the squad, the horde, every building within 110 px and the core if it is that close, so it is best killed far off. A brute and the Colossus, a far bigger brute, ignore the squad and march on the core. Otherwise a zombie bites the core once it reaches it, and the core shrugs off 60% of each bite. On the way it follows a flow field to the core that goes round the squad's walls while there is an open way and breaks through the cheapest wall when there is not. Zombie kills pay score up the same level ladder as the versus modes, and each kill adds scrap to the squad's shared bank. A player's level, perks and gun last for the whole run.

By day a player presses B for build mode and puts up a building on a 50 px grid cell within 600 px of the core and 250 px of themselves, on a cell clear of cover, the core, bodies and other buildings. The keys 1 to 5, or a click on the build bar, pick a wall for 20 scrap, a sentry for 70, a cannon for 180, a scatter for 90 or a mortar for 220. A building can come down by day for half its cost back, less the share of it that is worn. Buildings block bodies, zombies included, but the squad's bullets and grenades pass over them. A wall has 2000 hp, a sentry and a mortar 1000, a scatter 1200 and a cannon 1500. A walker bites a building for half its damage and a brute for its full damage.

A turret fires real rounds at the nearest zombie in range that no map wall or crate hides, and the squad's own buildings never block its view. Each turret prefers one kind of zombie and fires at the nearest of that kind first. A turret whose round would lose more than half to plating leaves plated zombies and the Colossus to heavier guns. A sentry reaches 420 px and fires seven 14-damage rounds a second, so it thins walkers. A cannon reaches 560 px and fires one 260-damage round every 2.2 seconds at brutes first. A scatter reaches 260 px and fires seven 11-damage pellets a shot at runners first. A mortar reaches 750 px and lobs a shell every 2.6 seconds over map cover to where its target will be when it lands, bursting for up to 160 damage across 120 px, plated first, so it breaks up packs. A sentry holds 120 rounds, a scatter 40, a cannon 10 and a mortar 8, and an empty turret stops firing. Its rounds never hurt the squad. A turret's kill pays the bank its scrap and pays the turret's builder the score, but counts as the turret's kill, not the builder's.

Holding use repairs the nearest damaged building or the core within 250 px at 80 hp a second, or reloads the nearest turret short of ammo, day or night. A worn turret is repaired before it is reloaded. The `Hold E to ...` hint names the job by the same rule the server follows. Repairing a building costs half its price for the share of it that is worn, so mending always costs less than taking it down and building it again, and the core costs 1 scrap per 5 hp. A sentry round costs 0.25 scrap, a scatter round 0.5, a cannon round 4 and a mortar round 5, and a full reload takes 2.5 seconds.

A player whose health runs out goes down instead of dying. A downed player crawls, cannot shoot and is ignored by the horde. A squadmate holding use within 70 px for 3 seconds revives them with 40% health. A downed player nobody revives within 25 seconds bleeds out. By night the Bastion sends them back 15 seconds later at the cost of survivors, 1 plus half the night's number rounded up, and with too few survivors left they wait for dawn. A human who joins or rejoins during the night sits out until dawn. Bots fight, revive, keep a dry turret firing and post behind the squad's walls, finding their way round the core and the squad's buildings. In a squad with no human they also repair and reload while no zombie is close and put up a fixed ring of turrets by day, the same distance out on every side, saving the scrap for each before they mend the core. With a human in the squad the bank is the humans': bots build nothing, keep enough for the dearest building, tend only with what is above that, and mend the core below it only when it falls under half at night.

### Last Squad

Last Squad, the BR room, is a battle royale for six squads of three. Each squad wears one of the six colors, and a squad's bullets and blasts spare its squadmates. A match plays one of the four versus maps, the next map in the rotation each time. Each squad starts together, at the spot farthest from the squads already placed.

The whole map is safe for the first 60 seconds. Then the safe circle closes in six phases, each new circle inside the last and centred on open ground: to 3200 px over 30 seconds, then after 45 seconds to 2500 px over 25, after 40 seconds to 1900 px over 20, after 30 seconds to 1300 px over 20, after 25 seconds to 700 px over 15, and after 20 seconds to nothing over 15. Outside the circle a player loses 2% of their max health a second in the first phase, then 3, 5, 8, 12 and 20%. The ring burns through armor and the spawn shield, and nobody outside it regenerates. `RING` in `src/shared/defs.ts` lists the phases.

A player whose health runs out while a squadmate is still standing is knocked, not killed. The knock pays the kill: 100 points, the bounty on a hunted player and the assists, as in the other modes. A knocked player crawls, cannot shoot, and has half their max health as a knocked pool that enemies' bullets and blasts take away to finish them, and the ring wears it down too. A squadmate holding E within 70 px for 3 seconds revives them with 40% health. Nobody in 25 seconds and they bleed out. A squad is out the moment nobody in it is standing, and its knocked players die with it. The kill feed tags each knock and names each finish, redeploy and squad that goes out.

Until the third phase closes, a dead player comes back beside a standing squadmate after 15 seconds, 10 seconds longer for each earlier death in the match, with their class gun and a spawn shield. When the third phase closes the HUD announces Last lives, and nobody redeploys after that. A dead player's camera follows a squadmate who is still up, then whoever killed them, then anyone left in the match.

Each phase drops supply 20 seconds into its wait, on open ground inside the next circle, and every minimap shows where it will land with a countdown from 10 seconds before. A drop is a crate with 300 health. Whoever breaks it jumps straight to their next level pick, and a player with every pick made gets full health, a full magazine and their ability back instead. Crates pay 25 points and stay broken for the rest of the match.

The last squad standing wins. Squads that go out in the same tick place by their kills, more kills placing higher. A squad that is out sees its result card: `#1 · Last squad standing` or `#4 of 6`, with your kills, knocks and revives. The next match starts 8 seconds after the win. A human who joins while redeploys are open takes the place of a bot in the squad with the fewest humans, where it stood and in the state it was in, so solo humans spread one per squad. A human who leaves then hands the place back to a bot. After the third phase closes a joiner watches until the next match seats them.

The pill shows the ring's phase and the time to its next move. Six columns of three pips under it track every squad: filled while up, hollow while knocked, faint while dead, and the squad's place once it is out. The world and the minimap shade everything outside the circle and dash the next one, and the minimap shows the drops and counts the ring down.

Bots play the versus brain, held to their squad. A squad's anchor is the middle of its standing members, pulled to 400 px inside the circle's edge, or to 60% of the radius on a circle under 1000 px. Each bot holds cover within 220 px of the anchor, facing the circle's centre, and drops any plan of its own that goes farther, so bots never chase gunfire or flank across the map. Bots set off for the next circle once the wait left is only 15 seconds more than the walk, and a bot caught outside walks back in whatever it is fighting. A bot revives a knocked squadmate within 900 px once no standing enemy is in sight, and shoots knocked enemies once no standing enemy is in sight. About half the squads go for each phase's drop when it lands within 1800 px of them.

## Layout

- `src/shared/defs.ts` holds every tuning number: weapons, armor, perks, cooldowns and world constants.
- `src/shared/maps.ts` registers the maps and each mode's rotation; a map's `size` is its side in pixels. The versus maps live in `src/shared/maps/`, each drawn as a text grid of 50 px cells: the west half only, since `src/shared/mapgrid.ts` turns it half way round for the east and merges cells into wall, spawn and crate rects. Its legend is `.` floor, `#` concrete, `S` sandstone, `P` planter, `c` crate, `R` red spawn (blue once turned), `F` FFA spawn, `X` both, and `A` zone A (B is the centre, C is A turned). Outpost, the zombies map, is still drawn in rects. `node scripts/map-lint.ts` checks every map for walls or crates past the edge, crates on walls, ground nobody can walk to, spawns a player cannot stand in, team spawns in sight of each other, a broken half turn and misplaced DOM zones, and lists each map's longest clear sightline against the longest gun's reach; `test/map-lint.test.ts` fails on any problem.
- `src/shared/protocol.ts` defines the wire messages and parses client input.
- `src/shared/wire.ts` encodes snapshots per connection: it rounds numbers and omits crates, leaderboard, zones and match while they are unchanged. The client rebuilds full snapshots from the last one it received.
- `src/shared/sim.ts` is the deterministic game simulation's entry point. `step` advances the world one tick, and the player commands add, remove, respawn and steer players. The rest lives in `src/shared/sim/`, one module per domain:
  - `world.ts` holds the world and player types, the seeded random source, map loading and spawn points.
  - `movement.ts` holds the collision geometry and player motion that the client also runs for prediction.
  - `stats.ts` holds perks, levels and score, and the effective stats they produce.
  - `combat.ts` holds bullets, damage, kills and lag compensation. Each input carries the server time of the world the client was drawing, and a shot first flies through that past, up to the client's measured round trip plus its render delay and never more than `MAX_REWIND_MS` back, so players hit what they aim at on screen.
  - `abilities.ts` holds the tier 3 abilities and the grenades, mines and gas they leave behind.
  - `modes.ts` holds the FFA, TDM, DOM, zombies and Last Squad rules and the round cycle.
  - `royale.ts` holds Last Squad: the ring's phases, knocks and finishes, redeploys, supply drops, squads going out, the win, seats for joiners and whom the dead watch.
  - `downed.ts` holds the downed life both squad modes share: going down, the revive and bleeding out.
  - `run.ts` holds the zombies run: its day, night and restart cycle, the night table's hordes, survivors, ready-up, the win, reinforcements, walls and scrap.
  - `build.ts` holds the wall rules, which the server applies and the client's build preview mirrors.
  - `horde.ts` holds the zombies themselves: the flow field to the core, their steps, bites and spacing.
  - `snapshot.ts` builds each player's culled view of the world, centred on the player a dead Last Squad player watches.
- `src/server/` contains rooms, bots, accounts and the HTTP and WebSocket server. `bots.ts` runs each bot's think. `bot/` holds its parts:
  - `arena.ts` builds each world's nav grid (`nav.ts`, A* over a grid of where a body fits) and cover points (`cover.ts`, spots beside walls and crates with the bearings they shield) once per wall layout.
  - `awareness.ts` turns a bot's snapshot into what it knows: enemies in sight, where it last saw others, and gunfire it heard.
  - `intent.ts` holds the personalities, each weapon class's fight range, and the one transition table between intents (patrol, take position, engage, peek and hide, reload in cover, retreat and heal, flank, search). An intent holds for a minimum time unless an interrupt fires.
  - `royale.ts` holds a Last Squad bot to its squad's anchor inside the ring, and has it revive and crawl.
  - `motor.ts` turns the intent into keys, aim and fire. No bot turns back within 400 ms of its last turn back. `aim.ts` holds the reaction and aim model: the gun turns toward where the bot wants it on a capped spring, the aim error drifts with a 400 ms time constant, and a bot fires only once its gun is on target.
- `src/client/` contains the browser client.

## Verify

```bash
npx tsc --noEmit
npm test
RUN=/tmp/skirmish-verify
.claude/skills/verify/scripts/launch.sh "$RUN"
.claude/skills/verify/scripts/doctor.sh "$RUN"
node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/drive.ts "$RUN"
node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/combat.ts "$RUN" tdm dom
.claude/skills/verify/scripts/cleanup.sh "$RUN"
```

`npm test` runs the simulation, protocol, client and end-to-end server tests. The scripts in `.claude/skills/verify/` prove behavior against a real, isolated server. `launch.sh` builds the client and starts a server with its own port and data dir. `doctor.sh` checks the server and bundle are current. `drive.ts` drives headless Chrome through the menu, login, movement, firing, latency and chat, and `combat.ts` checks objectives and damage in TDM and DOM. Both write `RESULT PASS` or `RESULT FAIL` to `$RUN/evidence/`. `cleanup.sh` stops the server and deletes its data. Set `CHROME` if Chrome is not at the default macOS path. See `.claude/skills/verify/SKILL.md` for details.

`node scripts/golden-replay.ts [hash]` guards refactors of `src/shared/sim.ts` and `src/shared/sim/`. It replays fixed-seed FFA, TDM and DOM matches with bots, scripted human players, abilities, perks, lag-compensated shots and round ends, and prints one SHA-256 hash of every snapshot. Record the hash before you change the simulation's structure. Then pass it as the argument after the change. The script exits with status 1 when the hashes differ. Bots drive these matches, so a change to the bot brain also changes the hash. The current hash is `dec94932061e9dc5f43c04ee915ae5d1004d95b109d273fd308ebeabf171872a`, taken with medals rescaled to carry more of the score, lifetime medals, the Ghost medal and the ladder raised for them, spawn shields, spread FFA spawns, bots that detour round a wall they are crawling along, kills that refuel the killer, streak shutdown and revenge bonuses, bots that lead to the intercept point and treat near misses as fire, per-class muzzle kicks, a load speed floor, retuned gun speed penalties, a +25% Lightweight, per-gun suppression, fast muzzles easing to slower cruising rounds, pinpoint planted snipers, suppression, moving spread and bloom on every automatic class, and heavier armor and gun movement costs, on the gun overhaul roster (breakpoints through heavy armor, bots fighting at their gun's reach), with the intent-driven bots turning their guns by hand and pacing their turns back, with class gun rules (moving spread, assault bloom, minigun spin-up, sniper view) and per-class attachment menus (the scripted local human plays a sniper so its ghillie pick is on its menu), on the 6000 px grid maps with their own size, 18-player rooms and the longer round targets. Zombies runs stay out of the replay so the hash holds; `test/zombies-determinism.test.ts` checks that a bot squad's run replays exactly from its seed.

`node scripts/bench-bots.ts` pits a scripted human against bots and prints the human's life, K/D and damage taken per minute. `node scripts/spasm-meter.ts [modes] [minutes] [seeds]` reads every bot's gun angle and position from snapshots sent through the wire encoder. It prints how far guns turn per tick, how often they reverse, how often bodies turn back and how soon after the last turn, and the brain decision behind each turn back. `node scripts/bot-trace.ts` writes an HTML replay of a bot match, tick by tick, with each bot's intent, goal and gun.

`node scripts/bench-royale.ts [--seeds 8] [--seed-base 1] [--maps oldtown,quarry,plaza,causeway] [--no-proxy] [--workers N]` plays bot-only Last Squad matches, one per seed and map, on worker threads, with one bot in one squad carrying a human's fourfold health unless `--no-proxy`. Per map and over all matches it prints whether each match reached a winner and how long it took, the share of deaths the ring dealt, squads the ring wiped, how many matches had their last fight in the last two phases, drops that two squads came within 500 px of, bot think time per tick, the proxy squad's wins, knocks and kills a minute in each phase, the gun stage of the players left standing, and the squads still in at each minute. Over 32 seeds and the four maps every match reaches a winner, at a median 4.75 minutes; the ring deals none of the 2233 deaths; knocks and kills rise from 1.4 a minute in the first phase to 4.0, 5.4, 7.4, 9.3 and 39.9; 92 of 128 matches have their last fight in the last two phases; and the proxy's squad wins 44 of 128. Over the first 16 seeds alone it wins 18 of 64, so judge the proxy over 32 seeds or more. `node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/royale-ui.ts "$RUN"` joins the BR room in headless Chrome, walks out of the ring until it knocks and then kills the player, and screenshots the start, the ring, the knock, spectating and the result card; run it on a scratch copy with a fast ring, as `.claude/skills/verify/features/last-squad.md` describes.

`node scripts/bench-zombies.ts [seeds] [squad] [runs]` plays zombies runs on fixed seeds to the core's fall or the Tide's dawn with a squad of four bots, with `1` a lone bot-brained player with a human's health, or with `mixed` the common real squad, one bot-brained player flagged human who follows the build plan and three bots. It prints whether each run won and on which night it ended, each night's length, core health bitten off, survivors left and buildings standing, and the win rate, mean core health lost, runs hurt and mean downs per night across the seeds. `node scripts/mortar-aim.ts` shells lone zombies marching in on the core from a mortar and prints the share of each shell's blast that lands on its target. Then, unless the third argument is `runs`, it holds a full horde of 200 zombies on the squad and prints server step time, whole-tick time and snapshot bytes, first with no buildings, then against a ring of 14 always-loaded turrets, against 70, and against those 70 a quarter each of every turret kind. `node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/zombies.ts "$RUN"` opens a squad on a launched server and plays its first night over `ws`. `node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/zombies-ui.ts "$RUN"` plays the same in headless Chrome through the menu, build mode, a sentry and night 1, and screenshots each state.

`node scripts/bench-maps.ts <FFA|TDM|DOM> [maps] [minutes] [players] [heatDir|-] [seeds] [capMinutes]` fills each map with bots (18 by default) on seeds 1 to `seeds` (2 by default). For each seed it plays one round under the mode's rules, capped at `capMinutes` (60 by default), then plays `minutes` more with the round held open. Per map it prints round length and, for TDM and DOM, the time each round took to reach the win score. From the open round it prints how soon the leader reaches each score, kills per minute, time from spawn to first contact, time between fights, bot life length, fight length, the share of fighting time spent in cover, the share of shots fired standing still, the share of deaths that came while low or outnumbered, and how far apart shooter and victim stood on each hit. It also prints tick time, split into bot think time and step time; raise `players` to time the bot brain in a fuller room. Last it prints the life figures over all maps and, for TDM and DOM, the mean and median time to the win score. With a heat directory it writes where players took damage and died. Pass `-` for no heat directory when you set `seeds` or `capMinutes`. `node scripts/map-overview.ts <outDir> [maps|all|none] [heat.json ...]` draws each map whole in a muted headless Chrome with the game's own ground and materials, and draws a bench's heat over it.

`node scripts/unused-exports.ts` lists every export that no file in `src/`, `test/`, `scripts/` or the verify scripts imports, and says whether its own module still uses it.

`node scripts/mock-server.ts 8787` with `node scripts/drive.ts http://localhost:8787 ./shots` is only a render check. Its fake server forces UI states (perk panels, death, the winner banner) so you can screenshot them, and proves nothing about gameplay. `node scripts/measure-bandwidth.ts [humans] [seconds] [room]` starts an isolated server, joins that many scripted clients, and prints bytes per second per client, snapshot arrival gaps and bytes per snapshot field. `node scripts/measure-lag-aim.ts [seconds] [lag:jitter ...]` starts a bot-free server, has headless Chrome tap the pistol at where it draws a scripted strafing target, and prints hit rate and damage per minute at each simulated latency.

## Deploy

The `Dockerfile` builds the client, drops dev dependencies, and runs the server as a non-root user on port 8080. Mount a volume at `/data` to keep accounts. The server saves and exits cleanly on SIGTERM.

```bash
docker build -t skirmish .
docker run -p 8080:8080 -v skirmish-data:/data skirmish
```

The server limits WebSocket connections and login attempts per IP address. Behind a reverse proxy every player arrives from the proxy's address, so they all share one limit. Set `TRUST_PROXY=1` to key the limits on the last `X-Forwarded-For` address instead, which is the one your proxy appends. Enable it only when exactly one proxy you control sits in front of the server, because clients can put any value they like at the front of that header.

```bash
docker run -p 8080:8080 -e TRUST_PROXY=1 -v skirmish-data:/data skirmish
```

### Fly.io

`fly.toml` runs the Docker image on one always-on machine with a volume at `/data`, `TRUST_PROXY=1` (Fly's edge is the one proxy in front), and a `/healthz` check. Keep it to one machine: rooms live in memory and the accounts file is not shared between machines.

```bash
fly launch --no-deploy --copy-config --name <your-app-name>
```

```bash
fly volumes create skirmish_data --size 1 --region iad
```

```bash
fly deploy
```

Change `primary_region` and the volume region to the one nearest your players. A deploy restarts the machine and disconnects everyone, so deploy at quiet times.
